e-portfolio 3 – Robotic Process Automation and Process Cybersecurity

Introduction

The application of Robotic Process Automation (RPA) is becoming increasingly popular with the intent of automating repetitive and rule-based tasks. As seen from a Business Process Management point of view, one can say that RPA can be useful since the process can be enhanced in its performance without the need to change the supporting information system. On the other hand, automation raises issues regarding cybersecurity, since software robots can engage with sensitive data, credentials, applications, and organisational systems. The artefacts that have been chosen to include in the e-portfolio provided a better understanding of both sides of automation.

Artefact 1 – RPA Implementation and Business Process Improvement

Gunawan et al. (2025) describe the example of RPA application within the business process of a student service process in a university. The authors demonstrate that businesses need to know the process, its problems or bottlenecks, and find out which activities within it may be automated before using RPA. In this case, Gunawan et al. (2025) showed how RPA could be used to automate repetitive administrative tasks and operate with existing information systems. The researchers indicated considerable improvements in processing time after the automation.
The chosen artefact helped me to grasp the connection between RPA and BPM. First, my main takeaway is that RPA should not be used automatically in the cases when any activity seems repetitive but needs analysis first. Secondly, it was also interesting that some tasks needed human intervention. That proves that BPM includes improving of the process, not replacing humans with software robots.

Artefact 2 – RPA Security Threats and Countermeasures

This artefact discusses cybersecurity risks associated with RPA. Among the identified risks are such risks as use of hard coded credentials, over-privileged bots, weak role based access control, insecure APIs, configuration weaknesses, privacy risks and monitoring gaps. Bots are able to interact with the applications of the organization and the sensitive data stored there and so, compromising a bot may impact other components of the business process. That is why the authors suggest the following controls: credential management, access control, monitoring and improved security practices.
I selected this artefact due to its ability to transform my perception about process automation. Previously, RPA was perceived as a means to increase the speed and reduce the repetitiveness of the tasks. However, this artefact helped me understand that process automation creates changes in the organization's risk profile. A bot can be provided with credentials and permissions similar to those of a human user. Therefore, bot's access needs to be controlled as well.

Artefact 3 – Cybersecurity and Process Mining

The study links cybersecurity and business processes directly. The researchers suggest an automatic framework with process mining which helps to explore the way the cyber attack can influence interconnected business processes and IT resources. Event logs are used to build business dependencies information and analyze the ways the consequences of the cyber attack can spread throughout the process. The framework was tested on procurement process data of an international transportation company.
The reason for choosing the artefact is the fact that process cybersecurity is more complicated than computer security. It means that from the BPM point of view, different activities, IT resources, information and people are interconnected. So, any security issue with any component of the process can affect other activities in the process. I found out that process mining is useful not only for identifying process inefficiencies but also for cybersecurity management issues.

Artefact 4 – RPA, AI and Agentic Automation

Here is an artefact which shows that traditional RPA evolves into agentic automation. UiPath presents an automation framework that can integrate AI agents, software robots, and humans into organisational processes. Instead of using only pre-programmed software robots for repetitive tasks, it incorporates various types of automation and human involvement into business processes. The UiPath enterprise agentic-automation platform was released in 2025 and focuses on orchestrating agents, robots, and people.
This artefact helped me to realize what future awaits for RPA and how it might transform into something more than simple rule-based automation. Also, I realized the importance of governance when automation becomes autonomous. When the software starts to make and trigger actions, there should be some limitations regarding permissions, accountability, and approval by humans. Later UiPath provides its governance materials and controls which include predefined scope of actions, centralized policies, permission models based on roles and auditing. In my opinion, this artefact links RPA to the process cybersecurity, as there is a need in increased governance and controls when there is more automation.

Overall Reflection.

By completing this e-portfolio, I was able to get an insight that RPA should be viewed from the perspective of Business Process Management instead of being treated as a tool in itself. Process mining would reveal activities which could be automated through RPA, while at the same time, automation of processes would present risks such as those relating to credentials, access, data and connected systems. The other lesson I gained from the process was that process mining would help in conducting cybersecurity analysis and that with more advanced AI-driven automation, governance becomes more important.
